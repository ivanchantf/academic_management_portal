Summary

  • Six secondary indexes cover five hot access paths. 5 queries move from full scans to index lookups, with a median p50 speedup of 14.6x and a maximum of 23.0x.
  • The largest wins are the per-student mitigation history (23.0x) and the teaching roster (16.95x). The first previously scanned 10,000 requests and sorted them; the second scanned 40,000 enrollments.
  • 6 queries keep their old plans and vary within ±14%. The course catalog's teacher subquery switches to a covering index but the overall query is flat.
  • The staff-wide mitigation list is the only material regression: p50 30.4 to 32.4 ms. The plan scans the new index and adds row lookups while the sort remains. The index stays because the per-student view is far more frequent and much faster.

Costs and trade-offs

  • Writes: enrollment inserts cost 3.31 to 5.31 ms per 500 rows (+60.4%, about +4 µs per row). This is the second B-tree maintenance cost. The test disables fsync; commit durability dominates in production.
  • Size: the database grows from 5.6 MB to 6.9 MB (+22.5%), scaling linearly with data.
  • Staff list: p95 rises from 46.7 to 62.9 ms. Pagination or keyset pagination is the follow-up once the table grows further.
  • Open issue: time-ticket windows store local input time but are checked against UTC CURRENT_TIMESTAMP, an eight-hour offset in Hong Kong. Indexes change the access path, not this semantic issue.

Method

  • Scale: 2,000 students, 200 staff, 300 courses, 40,000 enrollments, 10,000 mitigation requests, 4,000 time tickets, 6,000 overload requests. Fixed random seed.
  • Both databases come from the same create.sql. The baseline drops every idx_% index; the indexed copy runs database/indexes.sql. The rows are identical.
  • The 13 queries are copied from the NestJS services. Both sides use a 128 MB page cache and are warmed before timing. Five interleaved passes produce p50, p95, and p99. Only database latency is measured, not HTTP, auth, or serialization.
  • The write test interleaves both databases and disables fsync to isolate index maintenance. The database files live in a local temp directory to avoid cloud-sync I/O stalls.
  • Environment: macOS, SQLite 3.51.0, Python standard library sqlite3.
